A Brief History of The Wizard’s Castle

The Wizard’s Castle is an early text-based fantasy adventure and dungeon-crawling game written by Joseph R. Power around 1980, during the formative years of personal computer gaming.

Power developed the game while working with an Exidy Sorcerer computer at New Dimensions in Computing in East Lansing, Michigan. The fictional kingdom of N’Dic in the game’s introduction is a reference to the name of that business.

The game was written in BASIC and subsequently circulated among computer users in source-code form. Like many BASIC games of the period, The Wizard’s Castle was not tied permanently to one computer platform. Users could type in, copy, modify, and port the program to other BASIC-capable systems. As a result, numerous versions and adaptations appeared over the years.

The Castle

The game places the player inside the enormous subterranean castle of the wizard Zot.

The castle consists of eight levels, each containing an 8 × 8 grid of rooms:

8 × 8 × 8 = 512 rooms

The player’s objective is to explore the castle, locate the Runestaff, use it to obtain the legendary Orb of Zot, and escape alive.

Although the castle has a consistent geometric structure, its contents are randomized when a new game begins. Monsters, treasures, vendors, pools, books, crystal orbs, chests, stairs, warps, sinkholes, and other hazards can therefore appear in different locations from one game to another.

This procedural element gave The Wizard’s Castle considerable replayability despite the simplicity of its presentation.

Characters and Exploration

Players create an adventurer by choosing among four fantasy races:

* Elf
* Dwarf
* Man
* Hobbit

Characters are defined primarily by three attributes:

Strength (ST), Intelligence (IQ), and Dexterity (DX).

These statistics directly affect survival, combat, magic, and other interactions within the castle.

Players purchase their initial equipment before entering the dungeon and must then manage weapons, armor, gold, flares, a lamp, treasures, curses, and other resources while exploring.

Movement and interaction are performed entirely through short textual commands.

Monsters, Magic, and Treasure

The castle contains a progression of fantasy monsters ranging from kobolds and goblins to gargoyles, balrogs, and dragons.

Combat is turn based. Depending on the situation and the character’s abilities, the player may attack, retreat, attempt bribery, or use magic.

Characters with sufficient Intelligence can gain access to spells such as Web, Fireball, and Deathspell.

The dungeon also contains eight special treasures. Some are valuable in their own right, while others counter particular curses or conditions encountered during exploration.

Even apparently beneficial objects can be dangerous. Books may improve a character—or blind them. Chests may contain gold—or explode. Pools can increase an attribute just as easily as decrease it. Crystal orbs may provide useful visions, misleading information, or something considerably worse.

That mixture of risk, humor, and unpredictability is a large part of the game’s character.

The Orb of Zot

The central quest revolves around the Orb of Zot.

Simply discovering its location is not enough. The player must first obtain the Runestaff, then use its teleportation ability to reach the Orb correctly.

Crystal orbs scattered throughout the castle can sometimes reveal the Orb’s location, but their visions are not necessarily trustworthy.

Once the true Orb of Zot has been obtained, the player must return to the castle entrance and escape.

Leaving without it ends the adventure in failure.

An Early Procedural Dungeon Crawler

The Wizard’s Castle predates many conventions now associated with roguelikes and computer role-playing games, yet contains a surprisingly recognizable collection of them:

randomized dungeon contents, character statistics, equipment, exploration, hidden information, status effects, resource management, merchants, tactical combat, magic, treasure hunting, and a persistent objective tying the dungeon together.

Its interface is almost entirely text, but underneath that presentation is a fairly elaborate game system.

The game’s BASIC source also made it unusually portable. Copies were adapted to different computers and BASIC dialects, modified by individual users, and passed around through the early personal-computer community.

Consequently, there is no single experience of The Wizard’s Castle shared by everyone who encountered it. Different players may remember slightly different versions running on very different machines.

This Recreation

This project recreates Joseph R. Power’s The Wizard’s Castle for modern web browsers while preserving the character of the original game.

The goal is to retain the original 8 × 8 × 8 castle, rules, encounters, humor, terse command-driven gameplay, and monochrome terminal presentation while providing controls suitable for modern phones and tablets.

It is intended as a preservation-minded recreation of a fascinating piece of early personal-computer gaming history.
